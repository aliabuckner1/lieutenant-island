Nothing is actually measured at Wellfleet

NOAA station 8446613 is a subordinate station: no gauge, no sensor, and there never has been one. Every Wellfleet tide chart in every app is the Boston prediction multiplied by 1.05 and shifted fourteen minutes. You have been navigating by a number computed sixty miles away, and it only ever described the astronomy — never the weather.

The moon was never the problem

Lunar phase and perigee are already inside the prediction. Spring tides are not the surprise. What the chart cannot see is barometric pressure (−0.32 ft per 10 mb, almost exactly the textbook inverse barometer) and wind. Together with the sea-level trend those explain 58% of the error and cut the uncertainty from ±0.48 ft to ±0.31 ft.

So your rule of thumb was right — on an average day

A predicted 9.5 ft plus a typical surge lands almost exactly on the road's low point. That is why it worked for years. But the surge ranges from −0.6 ft to +2.0 ft, so the predicted height that actually floods the road swings between roughly 8.4 ft in a northeaster and 10.7 ft under a cold, dry, offshore high. One number could never cover that.

Read this before you trust the depths

The timing here is solid. The depths are not yet — the road's height comes from airborne lidar, good to about ±6 inches, and has never been checked against the actual water. Until it is, treat the first amber band as already closed and give yourself the margin. Water over a road is deeper and faster than it looks, and salt water ends cars.

Sources. Tide predictions: NOAA CO-OPS stations 8446613 (Wellfleet) and 8443970 (Boston). Live water level and barometric pressure: Boston 8443970. Wind history: NDBC buoy 44013, Massachusetts Bay. Wind forecast: NWS gridpoint BOX. Pressure forecast: Open-Meteo. Road elevation: USGS 3DEP lidar via The National Map. Vertical datum: NOAA VDatum (NAVD88 → MLLW = 6.14 ft at this location, ±0.39).

Method. The continuous Wellfleet curve is Boston's 6-minute prediction time-warped onto NOAA's official Wellfleet high and low waters — it reproduces them to within 0.002 ft. Surge is a least-squares fit of observed-minus-predicted high water on pressure, wind vector and year (n=3,258, R²=0.58), blended with the live Boston residual for the first eighteen hours.
